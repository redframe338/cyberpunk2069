# 11 — China and Kang Tao Design

> **Status: Outline.** China is Tier One; Kang Tao is Tier Two. Detailed in Phases 3–4 (`14`).

## 1. Identity

China — a rising power straddling the **Pacific** and **Eurasian** axes, defined by state–corporate
fusion. **Kang Tao** — the smart-weapons corporation, start position **state instrument** on the
corporate sovereignty ladder (`06`).

## 2. Central question

Reform, hardline, or corporate-fusion state? And: does Kang Tao remain the state's instrument or gain
autonomy?

## 3. Strategic role

- **Pacific axis:** contest Japan, Arasaka, and NUSA allies for Pacific control.
- **Eurasian axis:** compete (or cooperate) with the Neo-Soviet Union over Central Asia, energy, and
  industrial corridors (`08` §2.4).
- Kang Tao as a smart-weapons exporter feeding the corporate axis.

## 4. Paths (to be detailed)

- Reformist opening.
- Hardline centralization.
- State–corporate fusion (Kang Tao ascendant).

## 5. Open questions

- Depth of China's focus tree relative to the other Tier One powers.
- Kang Tao's relationship to the Neo-Soviet influence contest.
- China's stance in the European struggle (accommodation vs. rivalry).

## 6. Implemented 2069 strategic posture

China begins with 80 divisions organized around territorial defense, mobile response, and frontier
security. It is an armed commercial power rather than an expansionist one: AI strategy strongly
discourages starting wars and direct volunteer intervention while prioritizing free trade,
industrial production, and lend-lease support to states already at war.

China and the Neo-Soviet Union are mutual strategic rivals. Both sides receive a major negative
opinion modifier and AI containment/antagonism priorities, but China receives explicit negative
war and conquest priorities against the Neo-Soviet Union. Their opening conflict is therefore
economic, diplomatic, and technological rather than an automatic border war.
